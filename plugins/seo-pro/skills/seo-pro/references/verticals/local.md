# Local SEO and maps layer (practices L1–L22)

Covers visibility for searches with geographic intent: the Google local pack and Google Maps, Bing Maps / Bing Places (and Copilot local answers), Apple Maps via Apple Business, local organic results, and the directories and review platforms customers actually use. It includes profiles, location pages, location data, reviews, mentions, measurement and conversion. Read `README.md` in this folder first; it extends core practices 28, 38, 98, 99 and 106 and Bing practice B15.

Research snapshot: **18 October 2026**. Platform names, features and policies change by country; re-check the official links at the end before editing profiles in bulk.

**The thesis.** Local SEO means representing a real business accurately, connecting that entity to its locations and services, being useful to nearby customers, and measuring whether visibility produces calls, visits, bookings or sales. It does **not** mean adding cities to the business name, opening artificial listings or chasing a single map position.

Format: **Why** · **How** · **Expected result** · **Avoid** · **Verify**, with a confidence label from `../research-method.md` where the evidence is mixed.

## How local results work (and what isn't known)

- **Established:** Google says local results are based mainly on **relevance, distance and prominence**. Distance depends on the searcher's (actual or inferred) location; relevance on how well a profile matches the query; prominence on what is known about the business, including web information, links and reviews. Google states you **cannot pay or ask** for a better local ranking.
- **Not published:** the weights. Completing a profile field can improve accuracy, eligibility or conversion **without** changing position. Don't promise a ranking effect for a field.
- **"Map position" is not one number.** It varies with the searcher's point, the literal query, device, language, time, category, competition and surface (Google Search, Google Maps, Bing Maps, Apple Maps). A person can discover the business on one surface and convert on another.
- **Expert surveys are hypotheses, not the algorithm.** The Whitespark *Local Search Ranking Factors* (published Nov 2025: 47 practitioners scoring 187 signals in four areas, including AI search) ranks primary category, proximity and keywords in the business name near the top for Maps. That is informed opinion, not access to Google's system, and "keywords in the name" never licenses adding words that aren't part of the real name (L3).

## Surfaces and controls

| Surface | Control | What you manage | Metrics and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search + Maps | Google Business Profile | Eligibility, ownership, real name, categories, address/service area, hours, phone, website, services, products, photos, review replies | Profile *Performance* reports searches, views/interactions, calls, website clicks, direction requests and other available actions. Not unique people, not closed sales |
| Bing Maps / Bing Search / Copilot local | Bing Places for Business (bing.com/forbusiness, B15) | Claim or create, verify, import from GBP, fix duplicates, keep location and contact data | Features vary by country; verify ownership and re-check Maps after changes |
| Apple Maps and Apple surfaces | **Apple Business** (Apple Business Connect's features now live there, alongside Apple Business Manager and Essentials) | Place card: logo, photos, contact, hours, categories, supported actions | Check availability locally and on Apple devices; don't extrapolate Google impressions to Apple |
| Vertical directories and review sites | Portals relevant to the sector and country | Profile data, moderation policy, replies | Value depends on real use and audience; you don't need to be everywhere |

## Eligibility and the business entity

### L1. Classify the business model first — Critical
- **Why:** storefronts, service-area businesses (SABs), hybrids, individual practitioners, chains/franchises and online-only brands follow different rules. The wrong model produces suspensions or listings that can't rank.
- **How:** record for each location: does it receive customers at the address during stated hours? does staff travel to customers? is there permanent signage? is it one of several branches? Pick the model and read its row in L22.
- **Expected result:** a written inventory of locations, models, owners and accounts.
- **Avoid:** creating a local profile for an online-only brand with no in-person service; it may not be eligible at all.
- **Verify:** every location in the inventory has a model and an eligibility answer (L2).

### L2. Confirm eligibility before claiming or creating — Critical
- **Why:** Google requires profiles to represent a real business as it is known in the real world, with an accurate address or service area, and no more than one profile per business per location.
- **How:** check, in order: (1) customers are served at a real physical location **or** visited at theirs; (2) staff are present during the hours shown, if a storefront is shown; (3) signage where applicable; (4) it's a real business, not an entity created to rank for a keyword; (5) an existing profile for this business and location exists → **claim it, don't duplicate it**.
- **Expected result:** only eligible profiles; no duplicates.
- **Avoid:** virtual offices, unstaffed co-working desks, remote mail drops, residential addresses shown to the public when customers aren't received there.
- **Verify:** search the brand in Google Maps, Bing Maps and Apple Maps for duplicates and unclaimed listings.

### L3. The real name, not an SEO name — Critical
- **Why:** adding a city, category, phone, promotion or service to the name is against Google's guidelines and can lead to edits or suspension; Bing's equivalent guidance is the same (B15).
- **How:** use the name shown consistently on signage, the website, legal documents and customer communications. For a rebrand, document the change and follow the platform's rename or close-and-recreate procedure.
- **Expected result:** a name that survives edits and competitor reports.
- **Avoid:** "Smith Dental — Best Dentist Valencia"; repeating the trick on other platforms.
- **Verify:** name identical across profile, site header, legal page (106) and main directories.

### L4. Categories: specific primary, few secondaries — Critical · *Established for relevance; weights unknown*
- **How:** choose the **most specific** category for the core business; add a few secondaries only for activities really offered; keep the same category set for locations of the same type in a chain; re-check the country's current category list, which changes.
- **Expected result:** eligibility for the right queries and features.
- **Avoid:** categories as a keyword list, for services, attributes or neighbouring businesses; frequent category experiments.
- **Verify:** category matches what the location page (L12) describes.

### L5. Address, pin and service area — Critical
- **How:** a precise public address that meets the policy. Move the pin only to mark the real entrance, never to simulate another area. SABs **hide** the address and define real service areas. Each eligible branch gets its own profile; never several profiles for the same operation and address.
- **Avoid:** using service areas to appear "in" towns you don't serve; listings in areas where you don't operate. You can't choose where the searcher is; physical location keeps limiting likely map reach, and the website and prominence can help on broader local organic queries (L12–L13).
- **Verify:** pin position on satellite view; service areas match the operations team's real coverage.

### L6. Accurate profile data and attributes — High
- **Why:** accuracy prevents wasted journeys, calls out of hours and wrong expectations, even where a field has no demonstrated ranking effect. Google's AI answers in Maps now draw on this data (L8).
- **How:** a phone the business controls; the **location's** web page (not always the home page); regular **and** special/holiday hours; services and products; accessibility; payment methods; booking, menu or inventory links where supported. Update before, not after, changes in hours.
- **Avoid:** showing "open" when you aren't; stale menus, prices or hours.
- **Verify:** spot-check the profile against the master data (L16) monthly and before every holiday period.

### L7. Ownership and access governance — High
- **How:** profiles in a **company-controlled** account; least-privilege roles for agencies and staff; a trusted second owner; a record of who controls verification, phone and email. For chains: central governance, a named owner per location, an opening/closing/relocation procedure, and periodic permission audits.
- **Avoid:** sole ownership in an agency's or employee's personal account.
- **Verify:** list owners and managers for every profile; remove leavers.

### L8. Photos, posts and the Maps "Ask" feature — Medium
- **How:** current, own photos of exterior, interior, team, products and work; posts only when they carry real news. **Q&A is gone:** Google ended the Q&A API on 3 Nov 2025 and announced on 3 Dec 2025 that Questions & Answers is being removed from Maps; in many markets an AI **"Ask"** feature answers from profile data, reviews and the website (availability varies by country and category). So answer customer questions **on your website** and keep profile data, attributes, menus and services complete: those are the sources it reads.
- **Avoid:** misleading or stock imagery; claims that a fixed posting frequency is a ranking factor (no official support); building a strategy on the old Q&A box.
- **Verify:** ask the Maps "Ask" feature your top customer questions and log the answers (accuracy, source) with the AI observation log (A15).

## Reviews and reputation

### L9. A legitimate review programme — Critical
- **Why:** Google allows sharing an official review link or QR code; it prohibits paid reviews, incentives, asking only happy customers, pressure to change negative reviews, and posting from multiple accounts. Reviews can help prominence per Google's own guide, but asking for a review doesn't control its text, rating or publication.
- **How:**
  1. Deliver a consistent service; ask for feedback **neutrally**, after the experience.
  2. Use the official link/QR and send it to **all eligible customers**, not only those expected to rate well.
  3. No money, discounts, gifts, prize draws or free services in exchange for a review, a change or a removal.
  4. No **review gating** ("if you're happy, post; if not, contact us").
  5. Never write reviews for customers, dictate text, or use staff, family or suppliers as fake independent customers.
- **Legal context (not legal advice):** in the EU, Directive 2019/2161 requires traders who give access to consumer reviews to say whether and how they check that reviews come from real users; in the US, the FTC rule on reviews and testimonials (in force October 2024) bans buying fake reviews and conditioning incentives on sentiment. The UK CMA has its own guidance. Obligations depend on country and channel: link the primary source (`../research-method.md`).
- **Expected result:** a steady flow of genuine, recent reviews.
- **Avoid:** review-generation tools that filter by sentiment; contests "for reviews".
- **Verify:** the request flow, end to end, is identical for every customer.

### L10. Respond and report correctly — High
- **How:** reply with facts, empathy and a private route for personal data and incidents; never expose private information or discuss sensitive details; escalate reviews that **breach policy** via the platform's reporting tools.
- **Avoid:** reporting a genuine negative review just because it's negative; copy-paste replies that ignore the case.
- **Verify:** response time and share of reviews answered (L11).

### L11. Reputation metrics beyond the average — Medium · *Consumer surveys are self-reported*
- **How:** track new reviews per month, star distribution, recency, recurring themes, response time, repeated problems and share answered. Feed recurring complaints back to operations.
- **Avoid:** using the average alone, or a star threshold from a consumer survey (e.g. BrightLocal's US *Local Consumer Review Survey*) as a guaranteed conversion rule. Surveys measure declared behaviour in one market.
- **Verify:** a monthly table per location.

## Website and location pages

### L12. One page per real location — High
- **Why:** the profile shows data and actions; the website carries the detail, links and answers. Google uses diverse sources, including the business and contributors, for profiles and results.
- **How:** for each staffed location, a page with verifiable, specific content: local name and address, phone, hours and service status; services available **at that location**; how to get there, transport, parking, accessibility; team, photos, facilities or local cases; area served and real limits; links to booking, contact, directions, menu or inventory; `LocalBusiness` markup matching the visible content (L15). Link each profile to its own location page.
- **Expected result:** pages that answer local questions and convert.
- **Avoid:** dozens of auto-generated near-identical pages that swap only the place name (scaled content abuse; see core 26 and myths).
- **Verify:** compare any two location pages: what's different must be more than the town name.

### L13. Service areas without fake branches — High for SABs
- **How:** describe the areas you really serve and genuine work done there (projects, photos, response times). One page per area **only** when you have area-specific substance.
- **Avoid:** claiming a branch in a town where you have none; pages that imply an office.
- **Verify:** every area page reflects jobs actually done or coverage actually offered.

### L14. Local technical SEO — Medium
- **How:** indexable location pages, clear internal links from a "Locations" hub and the footer/contact page, correct canonicals; readable text for services, conditions and data (not only inside a map widget or iframe); `hreflang` when language/region versions are really equivalent (134–137); data identical across site, GBP, Bing Places, Apple Business and main platforms.
- **Avoid:** different phone numbers per crawler or engine that confuse users; if you use call tracking, keep a primary number consistent and the attribution controlled; duplicate location URLs under uncontrolled parameters (20).
- **Verify:** `curl -s URL | grep -i "tel:"` shows the same number as the profile; the auditor flags structured-data phones that aren't visible.

### L15. `LocalBusiness` structured data per location — Medium · *Myth if sold as a Maps ranking lever*
- **How:** the most specific subtype (`Dentist`, `Restaurant`, `AutoRepair`…), one entity per location on its page, with `name`, `address` (`PostalAddress`), `telephone`, `url`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `geo` when accurate, and `@id`/`parentOrganization` linking to the `Organization` (38). Values must match the visible page.
- **Expected result:** an unambiguous machine-readable description of each location.
- **Avoid:** marking up locations that don't exist; self-serving `Review`/`AggregateRating` on your own business to try for stars (not eligible, myths). Schema doesn't create GBP eligibility or Maps rankings.
- **Verify:** Rich Results Test / schema validator; the auditor's `LocalBusiness` checks.

## Location data, citations and other maps

### L16. A master source of truth per location — High
- **How:** one record per location: trading name, normalised address, phone, URL, hours (regular and special), coordinates, categories, platform IDs and status (open, temporarily closed, moved, closed). Every platform and page is generated from or reconciled against it.
- **Expected result:** fewer errors and faster changes across platforms.
- **Avoid:** each team editing profiles by hand with different data.
- **Verify:** a periodic diff between the master record and each platform.

### L17. Selective citations and directories — Medium · *Consistency: Established for users; listing counts: not supported*
- **How:** fix first the sources customers use and the main map platforms; then official bodies, sector directories, professional associations, chambers of commerce and local portals with real audience (98, 99). Create or update profiles individually and keep admin access. Clean up duplicates, closed businesses, moved locations and wrong phones.
- **Avoid:** "500 citations" packages with unknown sources, ownership and no way to correct or delete; obsessing over identical abbreviations.
- **Verify:** a list of priority platforms with owner and last check date.

### L18. Bing Places and Apple Business — High
- **Why:** Google isn't the only map. Bing Places feeds Bing Maps and Copilot local answers; Apple Business controls Apple Maps place cards, which matter on iPhone-heavy audiences. Their weight varies by country, sector and device.
- **How:** claim and verify in both (Bing can import from GBP: B15); same master data (L16); review supported actions (call, website, directions, booking) per platform.
- **Avoid:** assuming Google's metrics describe Apple or Bing.
- **Verify:** brand + city searches in Bing Maps and Apple Maps on a real device.

## Measurement

### L19. Measure per location and per surface — High
- **How:** a separate measurement sheet per location, keeping apart: local organic Google Search; local pack/map results; Google Maps; Bing Maps/Search; Apple Maps; directories and review platforms; web traffic and conversions. For every ranking observation record the **literal query, query point (coordinates, if the tool gives them), date, device, language, time, surface, category and competitors seen**.

| Stage | Possible metric | What it indicates | What it doesn't |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Eligible, verified profiles without duplicates; correct data | The ecosystem shows coherent information | That the profile is visible for every query |
| Discovery | Queries, views or impressions per platform | Exposure as the platform defines it | Unique people, intent or a universal position |
| Geographic coverage | Rank by query on fixed grid points | Spatial variation and opportunity areas | An objective rank for the whole area |
| Interaction | Website clicks, calls, directions, bookings, messages | Actions started from the profile | Answered calls, visits or completed sales |
| Web | Local sessions, landing pages, conversions, attributed calls | Site performance and capture | The causal effect of the profile without controls |
| Business | Attended bookings, tickets, sales, margin, new customers | Operating result | Which channel deserves credit without attribution rules |
| Reputation | New reviews, themes, response, reviewed sentiment | Perceived experience and failures | A direct, stable ranking signal |

- **Avoid:** "we're number 3" reports with no query, point, date or surface.
- **Verify:** every report row carries those fields.

### L20. Geo-grids are samples, not the truth — Medium
- **Why:** grids simulate searches from a lattice of points and show territorial variation; they are vendor data, dependent on query set, grid size and spacing, language, time, settings and scan frequency.
- **How:** use them to spot patterns and compare over time with the **same** configuration; confirm with real actions, Search Console, Profile Performance and sales.
- **Avoid:** "the grid went green, so the business grew"; one manual search from the office as the city's ranking.
- **Verify:** grid configuration stored with every scan.

### L21. Test design for local changes — Medium
- **How:** a hypothesis (e.g. "a location page with services, real hours and accessibility reduces wrong-number calls"); a baseline per location and query, long enough for the business's volume; **one** substantive change at a time, dated; comparable locations or pages as controls, noting differences in distance, competition and seasonality; track impressions, actions, answered calls and conversions, and lead quality; keep null results.
- **Avoid:** changing name, category, location, page, campaigns and review flow together; crediting a field edit for a lift that coincides with peak season, press coverage, a promotion or a new opening.
- **Verify:** a change log per location (shared with A15/A17 if AI answers are tracked).

## L22. Priorities by business model

| Model | Priority | Frequent mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Store / clinic / restaurant with premises | Eligibility, location and pin, category, hours, photos, products/services, reviews, location page, bookings, accessibility | Profile looks open or available when it isn't; outdated menu, prices or hours |
| Service-area business | Hide a private address, define services really provided, one profile per eligible base, genuine area pages and cases | Virtual offices or a listing in every town served |
| Individual practitioner | Follow the rules for public-facing practitioners, link practitioner and organisation profiles correctly, exact credentials and contact | Duplicate organisation, department and practitioner profiles without meeting the criteria |
| Chain or franchise | Central governance, consistent brand and categories, a named owner per location, feeds/API where available, opening and closing protocol | One profile for all locations, or unauthorised variations of name and data |
| Hybrid | Explain when customers are received on site and when staff travel; show the address only if customers are served there; real service area | Showing a residential address when customers aren't received |
| Online-only brand | National/e-commerce SEO (`ecommerce.md`) and brand profiles where eligible | A local listing with no in-person service |

## Local myths

| Claim | Assessment | Useful alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "City and service in the name improve ranking" | Not if they aren't part of the real name; breaches guidelines and risks suspension | Real name; use category, services, page and content |
| "Open a listing in every city you want to rank in" | Only for real, eligible locations | One valid profile per eligible location + useful area pages |
| "A hundred bought reviews will speed things up" | Against policy and potentially illegal; reputational and suspension risk | Neutral requests to real customers; fix operations |
| "Only ask happy customers" | Biased collection; can breach review rules | One neutral, uniform request for everyone |
| "Post once a week to move rankings" | No official support for a fixed frequency | Post real updates; measure actions |
| "A green geo-grid proves growth" | It shows that sample of points and queries | Combine coverage with calls, bookings, visits and sales |
| "All NAP citations are equal" | Quality, audience, country and reliability vary | Official, sector and customer-used sources first |
| "`LocalBusiness` schema ranks you in Maps" | Describes data; doesn't create eligibility or rank | Correct data, eligible profile, useful site, per-platform measurement |
| "Same reply to every review is enough" | Generic replies can reduce trust and don't fix causes | Specific, professional replies; fix the service |
| "The Business Profile replaces the website" | Not for complex services or multiple locations | Profile linked to each location page with measurable actions |
| "Answer questions in the profile's Q&A" | Q&A is being removed from Maps (Dec 2025) | FAQs on the website, complete profile data, monitor the "Ask" feature (L8) |

## 90-day sequence (not a ranking promise)

- **Days 1–15 — inventory and baseline:** locations, models, owners and accounts (L1, L7); eligibility, duplicates, suspensions, names, categories, addresses and areas (L2–L5); save the current state; build the master record (L16); choose queries and surfaces per location and record the baseline (L19).
- **Days 16–35 — data and operating risk:** fix hours, phone, address, status, website, services and secondary profiles (L6); access, verification, duplicates and permissions; priority directories and the most important location pages (L17, L12); a neutral, compliant review flow (L9).
- **Days 36–65 — usefulness and conversion:** improve location pages with specific, verifiable information; current photos, accessibility, booking, services and real FAQs (L8, L12); fix causes behind recurring review themes (L11); check Bing Maps, Apple Maps and sector platforms (L18).
- **Days 66–90 — measure and learn:** compare with the baseline, noting campaigns, seasonality and external changes; analyse coverage by query, not an average (L20); review call/lead quality, bookings and sales; pick the next test by impact × confidence × effort/risk (L21); document positive, null and adverse results before scaling to all locations.

## Executive checklist

- [ ] Each profile is an eligible business and location; no duplicates or fictitious offices.
- [ ] Ownership and permissions belong to the business.
- [ ] The name is the real name, without added keywords.
- [ ] The primary category represents the core activity.
- [ ] Address, pin and service area are real and policy-compliant.
- [ ] Phone, website, hours (incl. special hours) and services are correct per location.
- [ ] Location pages carry distinct, useful information for each location.
- [ ] `LocalBusiness` reflects visible content and isn't presented as a ranking guarantee.
- [ ] Review requests are neutral, authentic and incentive-free; a reply protocol exists.
- [ ] Relevant directories are controlled; no bulk low-quality submissions.
- [ ] Google, Bing and Apple are reviewed according to the audience and market.
- [ ] Measurement separates profile, map, organic, calls, leads, bookings and sales.
- [ ] Rankings are logged with query, point, date and surface.
- [ ] Factor claims are labelled official, observational or hypothesis.
